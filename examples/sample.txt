Persistence of Content-Addressed Storage: A 30-Day Field Study

Abstract. Decentralized storage networks promise durability without a central
custodian, but it is unclear whether content placed on such a network actually
remains retrievable over time. We measured the retrievability of 500 content-
addressed objects over 30 days across a peer-to-peer storage network. We find
that 94% of objects remained retrievable at 30 days, and that retrievability was
strongly associated with the number of independent nodes pinning the object.

1. Introduction. A recurring question for decentralized infrastructure is
whether data persists once the original uploader goes offline. We investigate
whether content-addressed objects remain retrievable over a one-month window,
and what predicts an object's survival.

2. Methods. We published 500 distinct objects and recorded, each day, whether
each object could be re-fetched by its content identifier from at least one node.

3. Results. Of the 500 objects, 470 (94%) were still retrievable on day 30.
Objects pinned by three or more independent nodes survived at a rate of 99%,
while objects pinned by a single node survived at only 71%. These results
indicate that redundancy of pinning is the dominant factor in persistence.

4. Related work. Our retrievability methodology follows the measurement approach
of Trautwein et al. (2022), "Design and Evaluation of IPFS," which characterized
content routing latency across the same network (DOI 10.1145/3544216.3544232).

5. Conclusion. Content-addressed storage can be durable, but durability is not
automatic: it is a function of how many independent nodes choose to retain a copy.
